Searching for a home automation company in Broward County turns up a strange mix. National brands with a call centre in another state, one person operations working out of a van, low voltage contractors who mostly do commercial work, and big box installers who will happily mount a doorbell and leave. All of them can technically do the job. Very few of them will plan it for the house you actually own.

This guide is about choosing between them here, in Broward specifically, because the housing stock in this county creates problems that generic advice does not cover. If you want the general vetting checklist, licensing, references, written quotes, and the warning signs, that is in our guide on how to choose a local installer. This one picks up where that leaves off.

Broward is really two housing markets

Draw a line somewhere around the Turnpike and the county splits in two, and the split matters enormously for this kind of work.

East of it you have the older stock. Oakland Park and Wilton Manors are full of 1950s and 1960s one story ranch houses. Fort Lauderdale neighbourhoods like Victoria Park mix restored midcentury homes with new townhomes on the same street. These houses frequently have no neutral wire at the switch box, no common wire at the thermostat, and plaster or block walls that decide what is realistic before anyone opens a catalogue.

West of it, Parkland, Weston, and much of Pembroke Pines are newer and larger, often two storey with two air conditioning systems. Different problem entirely: the wiring is fine, but the house is big enough that wifi runs out in the back bedroom and the upstairs system fights the downstairs one.

A company that only works one side of that line will tend to specify the same solution everywhere. Ask which they see more of.

Ask who is actually holding the electrical license

This is the question that separates companies fastest, and most homeowners never ask it.

Switch work, thermostat wiring, and anything at the panel is regulated electrical work in Florida. Some companies employ their electricians. Some, like us, work as the merchant of record and contract a licensed electrician to perform the regulated work, which means you know exactly who is accountable for which part. Some quietly have an unlicensed installer do it and hope nobody asks.

You are entitled to a straight answer about who will be doing the electrical portion and under what license. A company that gets vague at that question has told you something useful.

Condo and association rules are not an afterthought

A very large share of Broward homes sit under an association, and this is where projects stall.

Buildings in Pompano Beach like the Palm Aire and Cypress Bend towers, the 55 and over communities of Tamarac and Coconut Creek, and the villages around Margate each have their own rules about what may be attached to an exterior wall, a shared walkway, or a front door. A doorbell on a common corridor is often an approval item. Anything touching the building envelope usually is.

The right question is not whether a company can install the device. It is whether they have dealt with your kind of association before and will plan around what is permitted. In a condo, most of the good work happens inside your own walls anyway, and a company used to single family houses will not always know that.

A plan before a price, not a price before a plan

The most reliable signal of a company worth hiring is the order they do things in.

A quote produced over the phone, before anyone has seen your rooms, is a guess dressed up as a number. It is also how projects end up with change orders once someone opens the first switch box and finds no neutral. A company that wants to look at the house first, room by room, is a company that intends the final number to hold.

That is the reasoning behind our own free smart home floor plan. We map the rooms, specify what goes where, and show you the full price before you commit to anything. If you do not like it, you keep the plan and owe nothing. Whoever you end up choosing, insist on that sequence.

Check they will still be there afterwards

Smart home work is not finished on install day. Devices get renamed, families change how they use rooms, someone adds a lamp, an app updates and moves a setting.

Ask what happens in month three. Is there a person who knows your house, or a support queue in another time zone? This is the single biggest practical difference between a local Broward company and a national brand, and it does not show up anywhere on the quote.

Beware of anyone selling you a package

If the first thing a company shows you is a tiered package, be careful. Packages are built around what is convenient to stock, not around your floor plan. A three bedroom ranch in Margate and a two storey house in Coral Springs genuinely need different device counts, different switch types, and different numbers of voice points.

The useful version of this conversation sounds like a walkthrough, not a menu. Which rooms do you pass through with your hands full. Where does the family actually gather. Which lights does nobody ever turn off.

Questions worth asking any Broward company

  • Who performs the regulated electrical work on my project, and under whose license?
  • Do you see more older east Broward homes or newer western builds?
  • Have you worked inside my association or building type before?
  • Will you check for a neutral wire and a common wire before quoting me?
  • Do I get a written plan of the whole home, and do I keep it if I say no?
  • Who do I call in three months when something needs changing?
  • Which platform are you standardising me on, and why that one?

On the last one, we build on Amazon Alexa and we say so upfront. A company that will not name a platform tends to leave you with several apps that do not talk to each other.

What we would do in your house

Most Broward projects we take on start in one of three places. Smart lighting installation, because it is the thing people use every day and the easiest to get wrong. Smart thermostat installation, because the AC here runs most of the year and the schedule is usually still the builder default. Or Alexa setup and routines, when the devices are already in the house and simply do not work together.

From there it grows into whole home voice control and, for a lot of families, smart lock and doorbell installation at the front door. You can watch real routines running in real rooms on our routines page, which is a better guide to whether this suits you than any brochure.
